## Scotty's Castle
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/learn/historyculture/scottys-castle.htm — fetched 2026-09-17
- "Scotty's Castle area is closed until further notice following a significant flood, and later fire."
- Closed since the **October 2015 flood**, which left "mud and debris up to 10 feet deep"; a later fire

  by advance reservation while the castle itself is closed.

## Titus Canyon Road (detail)
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/titus-canyon.htm — fetched 2026-09-17 (page "Last updated: January 6, 2018")
- "The most popular back-country road in Death Valley National Park."
- Vehicle: "Two-wheel-drive, high-clearance recommended; four-wheel-drive may be needed after adverse
  weather conditions. Two-way section from west OK for two-wheel-drive, standard clearance vehicles."
- Distance: **27 miles**; last 3 miles on the west end are two-way. Time: **2 to 3 hours**.
- Start: Nevada Highway 374 (Daylight Pass Road), 2 miles east of the park boundary. One-way westbound.
- Highlights: Red Pass (5,250 ft), Leadfield ghost town (boomed 1926–27), Klare Spring petroglyphs and
  bighorn sheep, and final **1.5 miles of narrows** where "walls squeeze down to less than 20 feet apart."
- "Infrequently patrolled, summer travel not advised. Canyon prone to flash flooding."

## Racetrack Playa
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/the-racetrack.htm — fetched 2026-09-17
- "The road to the Racetrack is rough, and good tires, 4x4 and high clearance are usually required.
  Standard rental vehicles are not recommended, and often get flat tires."
- "**Drive time from Furnace Creek is at least 3.5 hours each way.**" No cell coverage.
- Road begins near Ubehebe Crater; 20 miles to Teakettle Junction, then on to the playa.
- Moving-rock mechanism: rain of ~1/2 inch plus thin ice (<5 mm) and light winds (~10 mph); rocks
  move a few inches per second and have travelled as far as 1,500 feet.

## Backcountry driving rules & vehicle prep
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/backcountryroads.htm — fetched 2026-09-17 (last updated April 1, 2025)
- "Death Valley has more miles of roads than any other national park." **Over 93% of the park's
  3.4+ million acres are roadless Wilderness**; nearly 1,000 miles of paved and dirt roads.
- "Travel prepared to survive, with **2 full size spare tires** and extra food and water."
- Off-road tires rather than highway tires; carry fix-a-flat or plug kit, 12-volt compressor, lug wrench,
  complete jack. Snow/ice at higher elevations may require tire chains. Top off fuel before any trip.
- Water: "at least a gallon per person per day. A 5-gallon container of water is standard emergency backup."
- Breakdown: "It is best to stay with your vehicle... Leave the car's hood up and/or mark the road with a
  large X visible to aircraft... **cell phone reception is non-existant in most areas of the park**.
  Towing charges are high and AAA often doesn't cover tows on dirt roads."
- "Travel in a group of two or more 4WD vehicles in remote areas."
- Driving off designated roads is prohibited. ATVs, dirt bikes, golf carts and green-sticker vehicles
  are prohibited on all park roads. Wilderness boundary is 50 ft from the centerline of most dirt roads.
- **Rental cars:** "Most vehicle rental agreements restrict vehicles to paved roads."
  **Farabee's Jeep Rentals** has an outlet in Furnace Creek with vehicles outfitted for backcountry roads.
- Road mileage (FAQ page, fetched 2026-09-17): "more than 300 miles of paved roads, 300 miles of improved
  dirt roads and several hundred miles of unmaintained 4x4 roads."
