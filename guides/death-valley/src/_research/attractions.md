# Attractions — Death Valley NP
All figures below are quoted from official NPS pages. Every entry gives its source URL and fetch date.
NPS repeats one rule verbatim on nearly every hiking page: **"HIKING NOT ADVISED AFTER 10 AM IN THE SUMMER."**

## Ranking basis
NPS tags its own pages with visit-length tiers. Pages tagged **"1-3 hour visit highlight"**
(the park's own do-not-miss list): Badwater Basin, Artists Palette, Zabriskie Point, Devils Golf Course.
Pages tagged **"half-day visit park highlight"**: Dante's View, Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes, Harmony Borax Works.
Tagged **"longer visit park highlight"**: Ubehebe Crater.
The park FAQ's own two-hour itinerary is: "Devils Golf Course, Badwater, and... Artists Drive on the return
trip... Add a stop at Zabriskie Point if you are continuing east, or stop at the sand dunes if you are
heading west." (https://www.nps.gov/deva/faqs.htm, fetched 2026-09-17)

## 1. Badwater Basin  (type: viewpoint / salt flat walk)
Sources: https://www.nps.gov/places/badwater-basin.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, page last updated Jan 7, 2026);
https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/visit-badwater-basin.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- **Lowest point in North America, 282 ft (86 m) below sea level.**
- Salt flats "cover nearly 200 square miles (518 square km)"; mostly sodium chloride plus calcite,
  gypsum and borax. Geometric salt polygons form as groundwater rises and evaporates.
- Round Trip Length: **1 mile (1.6 km) to edge of salt flat**; Round Trip Time: **40 minutes**;
  Difficulty: **Easy**; Elevation Gain: Flat; Trail Type: "Boardwalk then route, out and back."
- Best views of the salt polygons require "an easy **1.5–2 mile (2.4–3.2 km) roundtrip walk**."
  (The thingstodo page separately says salt polygons begin about 1/4 mile out — both figures are official.)
- Location: "Badwater Road, **30 minutes (17 miles/27 km) south of Furnace Creek**."
  CONFLICT: the park FAQ page says "18 miles south of the Furnace Creek Visitor Center on the Badwater
  Road (CA 178)." Guide uses the places-page figure (17 mi / 30 min) and does not state both.
- Parking: paved lot with large spaces for RVs and buses. Vault toilet in the lot.
  **ADA-accessible ramp down to a wooden boardwalk**; boardwalk is "older wood, so it is not entirely
  level"; salt surface beyond is firm, "like hard packed sand."
- Look for the **sea level sign** high on the Black Mountains cliffs to the east; Telescope Peak
  (**11,049 ft / 3,368 m**) is "over two miles (3.2 km) above you" to the west — "Nowhere else in
  America can you see such a dramatic vertical relief over such a close distance."
- "Sunrise and Sunsets from here are usually spectacular." Summer: not recommended outside the vehicle
  longer than 15 minutes. Pets not allowed. Named for a mule that refused to drink the (salty, not bad) spring.
- **No boating / no flotation devices anywhere in the park** (posted on the Badwater page re: Lake Manly).

## 2. Artists Palette via Artists Drive  (type: scenic-drive)
Sources: https://www.nps.gov/places/artists-palette.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, last updated Oct 10, 2024);
https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/tour-artists-drive.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- "The **most popular scenic drive in the park**" — a **one-way, nine mile (14.5 km) paved road**.
- **Vehicle length restriction: 25 ft (8 m)** "due to dramatic dips and sharp turns" / "sharp bends and large dips."
- Entrance: "Left turn off Badwater Road, **8.5 miles (13.7 km) south of the Badwater/CA-190 junction**."
  One-way northbound; you exit further north on Badwater Road.
- Duration: **25–45 minutes**. NPS: "allow approximately 30 minutes to detour from Badwater Road."
- Colors (red, orange, yellow, blue, pink, green) come from "volcanic deposits rich in compounds such as
  iron oxides and chlorite."
- Light: "beautiful any time of day, but the colors are **most dramatic during afternoon light**."
  The places page adds that sunrise and sunset "provide additional shadows and changing light."
- "This is a popular route for cyclists, so watch for bicyles and pedestrians in the road."
- "No maintained trails exist along this loop, several pull outs provide safe parking areas."
  Artists Palette view "can be seen from the parking area." Vault toilet at Artists Palette.
- Pets allowed with restrictions (roads/developed areas only, 6 ft leash).
- Parts of **Star Wars Episode IV: A New Hope** were filmed around Artists Palette.

## 3. Zabriskie Point  (type: viewpoint)
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/zabriskie-point-scenic-viewpoint.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, last updated Aug 15, 2026)
- NPS "Significance: Must-see highlight." "Some of the most photographed" views in the park.
- "A **15 minute drive east on Highway 190 from Furnace Creek Visitor Center**."
- "From the parking lot, the point is a **¼ mile (400 m) walk up a hill on a paved trail**." **Wheelchair accessible.**
- **Manly Beacon** "rises to an elevation of **823 ft (251 m)**" — named for 49er William Lewis Manly.
  "Especially magnificent when viewed at sunrise or sunset."
- Named for Christian Zabriskie of the Pacific Coast Borax Company.
- The **Badlands Loop Trail** starts from the Zabriskie Point parking lot; longer routes connect to
  Gower Gulch, Golden Canyon and Red Cathedral.
- Amenities: parking (auto + bus/RV), vault toilet, interpretive exhibits, trailhead.
- Listed by the park FAQ among "the most popular places to see sunrise" (with Dante's View and the dunes)
  and also among the best sunset spots.

## 4. Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes  (type: dunes)
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/mesquite-flat-sand-dunes.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, last updated Oct 3, 2023)
- Location: "**Just east of Stovepipe Wells Village off CA-190**." (Stovepipe Wells is 24 mi west of Furnace Creek.)
- Hike to Tallest Dune: Round Trip **2 miles (3.2 km)**; Time **1.5 hours**; Difficulty **Easy/Moderate**;
  Elevation Gain **185 ft (65 m)**; Trail Type: **"No official trail"** — open for cross-country exploration.
- Parking: paved lot with pull-through spaces for buses and large RVs. Vault toilets in the lot.
- "Known for **dramatic shadows at sunrise and sunset**, as ripples and edges in the dunes stand out in
  greater contrast. They are also a great place to observe Death Valley's famous dark night skies."
- Dune formation needs a sand source (eroding mountains to the north), wind, and a barrier (mountains to the south).
- Named for the mesquite trees; mesquite bean pods are "a traditional food of the Timbisha Shoshone."
- Habitat for nocturnal kangaroo rats and sidewinder rattlesnakes — "watch your step... do not disturb burrows."
- **Pets are not allowed on the dunes.** "HIKING NOT ADVISED AFTER 10 AM IN THE SUMMER."

## 5. Furnace Creek area  (type: area)
Sources: NPS structured data feed https://www.nps.gov/deva/structured_data_deva.json;
https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/visit-the-furnace-creek-visitor-center.htm;
https://www.nps.gov/places/harmony-borax-works.htm;
https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/lodging.htm; https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/directions.htm
— all fetched 2026-09-17
- **Furnace Creek Visitor Center**: open **8:00 am – 5:00 pm, every day of the year including holidays**
  (structured data feed, operatingScheduleID 5624). Coordinates N 36°27.70, W 116°52.00 — **there is no
  street address for the park or the visitor center.** Pay entrance fees, talk to rangers, 20-minute park
  film, museum exhibits, Junior Ranger program, Death Valley Natural History Association bookstore
  (light snacks/sandwiches/water for sale). ADA assisted-listening and audio-description devices available.
  **Public Wi-Fi 24 hrs/day at the visitor center.** Famous outdoor thermometer.
- **Harmony Borax Works**, 1 mile (1.6 km) west of Furnace Creek on CA-190: Round Trip **0.4 mi (0.6 km)**,
  **30 minutes**, **Easy**, 50 ft gain, **gravel/paved loop, wheelchair accessible**, no restrooms.
  Adobe ruins and a 20-Mule Team wagon with interpretive panels. Built by William T. Coleman; processed ore
  from late 1883/early 1884; 40 men produced three tons of borax daily, most of the workforce Chinese
  laborers; closed 1888. On the National Register of Historic Places since December 31, 1974. Borax was
  hauled **165 miles to Mojave** by the famous twenty-mule teams.
- **The Oasis at Death Valley** (Xanterra): The Inn at Death Valley and The Ranch at Death Valley —
  both open all year; lodging, food, and (at The Ranch) fuel. Four EV chargers at each.
- **Fuel**: The Oasis at Death Valley sells two grades of gasoline and diesel.
- **Timbisha Shoshone village** is at Furnace Creek — Sunset Campground's access road is "across from the
  Timbisha Shoshone village" (NPS structured data). The Timbisha Shoshone are the park's Indigenous people;
  Ubehebe Crater is "an important cultural site for the Timbisha Shoshone people."
- **Furnace Creek Golf Course** — at The Ranch; "the world's lowest elevation course at 214 feet below
  sea level" (oasisatdeathvalley.com, fetched 2026-09-17).
- Furnace Creek Campground is beside the visitor center, at 190 ft below sea level.
- Furnace Creek Ranch is where the **world record 134°F (57°C)** was recorded on July 10, 1913.
- A small public airport at Furnace Creek (no fuel, bring your own tie-downs).

## 6. Dante's View  (type: viewpoint)
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/dantes-view.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, last updated Jan 11, 2024)
- "Towering **5,575 ft (1,699 m) above Badwater Basin**, on the ridge of the Black Mountains."
- "A **45 minute drive from Furnace Creek**." Wheelchair accessible; parking, vault toilet, exhibits, trailhead.
- "Sunrise and sunset are especially magnificent here"; a short hike along the ridge to the north finds solitude.
- **Mt. Perry** continuation: "an **8 mile round-trip** journey with approximately **1,200 ft (366 m)** of
  elevation gain." (This is the ridge walk often called Dante's Ridge.)
- Night: "Death Valley National Park is an International Dark Sky Park... a visit during a new moon will
  provide a humbling glimpse of the Milky Way"; at full moon "the salt flats below glow a ghostly white."
- The view is Mos Eisley in Star Wars. Quote from Charles Brown of Shoshone, 1926: "I don't pay much
  attention to scenery, but I know one view that made me stop and look."
- UNVERIFIED: a vehicle-length restriction on the final steep grade to the viewpoint is widely reported
  but is NOT stated on the NPS page fetched; the guide does not assert one.

## 7. Golden Canyon (with Red Cathedral / Gower Gulch)  (type: hike)
Sources: https://www.nps.gov/places/golden-canyon.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, last updated Oct 10, 2024);
https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/hiking.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Location: "**2 miles (3.2 km) south of CA-190 on Badwater Road**." Paved lot with RV/bus spaces; vault toilet.
- Round Trip Length: **2–7.8 miles (3.2–12.6 km) depending on route**; Time: **1.5 to 4.5 hours**;
  Difficulty: **Easy/Moderate**; Elevation Gain: **535 ft to 834 ft (163 m to 254 m)**;
  Trail Type: official trail, loop or out-and-back.
- Base hike is 2 mi round trip with "one 3 foot (1 m) rock scramble."
- **Red Cathedral** adds 0.5 miles (0.8 km) to "the vertically fluted walls of a natural amphitheater"
  and "involves scrambling and squeezing/crawling through rock spaces."
- Full **7.8 mile (12.6 km)** loop goes to Zabriskie Point and back via **Gower Gulch**.
  (The hiking table separately lists "Gower Gulch Loop — 4.3 mi (6.9 km), Moderate.")
- Free maps at Furnace Creek Visitor Center; there is a Golden Canyon audio tour in the NPS App.
- No restroom along the trail. "HIKING NOT ADVISED AFTER 10 AM IN THE SUMMER."

## 8. Mosaic Canyon  (type: canyon / hike)
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/mosaic-canyon.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, last updated Aug 15, 2026)
- Location: "**the end of the 2.3 mile (3.7 km) unpaved Mosaic Canyon Road** located near Stovepipe Wells
  Village across from Stovepipe Wells Campground." The access road is "typically passable by sedans."
- Round Trip Length: **4 miles (6.4 km)**; Time: **2.5–3 hours**; Difficulty: **Moderate**;
  Elevation Gain: **1,200 ft (366 m)**; Trail Type: out-and-back route.
- "Hikers are greeted almost immediately by slick, winding narrows," polished smooth by flood-borne debris.
- "Many people stop at the **boulder jam approximately 1.3 miles (2 km) up the canyon**"; beyond it the
  route ends at an **impassable 25 ft (7.6 m) dryfall** in a dramatic amphitheater.
- Named for the **mosaic breccia** — "rock created by the natural cementing together of older smaller pieces of rock."
- **"Mosaic Canyon is the site of frequent flash floods (do not enter if there is a chance of rain!)"**
- Parking: large gravel area; "Buses and large RVs not recommended." Closest restroom is Stovepipe Wells Village.
- NPS hiking table rates it "Moderate to Difficult" and notes "Many hikers choose to hike to the first set
  of beautiful canyon narrows less than 0.5 miles" in.
- Mosaic Canyon Road is a **day-use-only road** (no roadside camping) per backcamp.htm.

## 9. Devils Golf Course  (type: viewpoint)
Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/check-out-devils-golf-course.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, last updated Dec 20, 2020)
- "An immense area of rock salt eroded by wind and rain into jagged spires. So incredibly serrated that
  'only the devil could play golf on such rough links.'"
- "Listen carefully and you'll hear sounds like tiny pops and pings -- the sound is literally billions of
  tiny salt crystals bursting apart as they expand and contract in the heat."
- Access: "the dirt road and parking area is made from **compacted gravel suitable for most vehicles**."
- "**Best viewed from the parking area.** The sharp salt formations can be dangerous to try to walk around
  on and are also fragile and can be easily damaged." Not a camping location. Pets not allowed.
- Season: "Winter, Spring, Fall... From mid-May to mid-September, this area can be extremely hot with
  temperatures reaching 120 F or higher." Time of day: any — good for sunrise, sunset and night sky.
- "Leaving golf balls out in Wilderness areas is littering."
- Off Badwater Road between Artists Drive and Badwater Basin. NPS does not state the spur-road mileage
  on this page — the guide says "short gravel spur off Badwater Road" rather than inventing a number.

## 10. Ubehebe Crater  (type: viewpoint / hike)
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/ubehebe-crater.htm (fetched 2026-09-17, last updated Jul 12, 2026)
- A **maar (explosion pit) volcano**: "molten lava came into contact with ground water, creating steam
  which eventually produced enough pressure to explode through the earth."
- Largest and youngest of about a dozen craters in the Ubehebe volcanic field; **estimated 2,100 years old**.
- **"approximately 0.5 miles (0.8 km) in diameter, and ranges from 500-777 feet (152-237 m) deep."**
- Rim hike: Round Trip **1.5 miles (2.4 km)**; Time **1 hour**; Difficulty **Moderate**;
  Elevation Gain **500 ft (152 m)**; official loop trail. "Exposed edges! Not good for those with a fear
  of heights." Usually walked counter-clockwise; **Little Hebe Crater** comes into view at 0.5 mi.
- "**Allow an hour drive time from Furnace Creek** and come prepared for windy conditions."
- Location: "8 miles (13 km) west of Scotty's Castle." Paved lot big enough for buses and large RVs.
  **No restrooms** — nearest are at the unstaffed Grapevine Ranger Station 5 miles (8 km) east.
- "Many visitors enjoy hiking into the depths of the crater, but remember that the hike back up is a
  difficult slog in ankle-deep debris, and should not be attempted during hot summer months."
- "Afternoon and sunset are particularly grand times to visit."
- "Please visit this area with respect, as it is an important cultural site for the Timbisha Shoshone people."

## Also considered (honorable mentions, not in the top 10)
- **Natural Bridge** — https://www.nps.gov/places/natural-bridge.htm (fetched 2026-09-17):
  RT **1 mi**, **45 minutes**, **Easy**, **86 ft (26 m)** gain, no official trail (walk up the wash).
  Trailhead at the end of unpaved Natural Bridge Road, **13.5 miles (22 km) south of CA-190 off Badwater Rd**;
  "typically well-maintained and accessible by most vehicles" but washouts and deep ruts can occur.
  Bridge rises ~**35 ft (11 m)** above the canyon floor and is about 35 ft thick; made of cemented alluvial
  deposit, not sandstone. Vault toilet in the lot.
- **Twenty Mule Team Canyon** — https://www.nps.gov/places/twenty-mule-team-canyon.htm (fetched 2026-09-17):
  a **2.5 mile (4 km) one-way dirt road** winding between yellow hills, "just east of Zabriskie Point."
  "Usually passable by most vehicles" — check conditions first. Roadside camping prohibited.
  Dogs may be walked on this road.
- **Titus Canyon Road** — see roads-conditions.md. 27 mi, 2–3 hr, one-way from NV-374.
  **Closing for repairs Oct 1, 2026 – Sep 30, 2027**, so it is deliberately kept out of the top 10.
- **Racetrack Playa** — see roads-conditions.md. At least 3.5 hours' drive each way from Furnace Creek,
  4x4 + high clearance + good tires; rental cars not recommended.
- **Salt Creek Interpretive Trail** — NPS hiking table (fetched 2026-09-17): **0.5 mi (0.8 km)**, flat,
  **Easy**, "ADA accessible boardwalk." Home of the endemic Salt Creek pupfish, best seen in spring
  (park FAQ: "If you visit Salt Creek in the springtime you can see pupfish").
  UNVERIFIED: current physical condition of the boardwalk — it is not named on the conditions page in
  either the open or closed lists, and there is no dedicated NPS status page that resolved.
- **Darwin Falls** — NPS hiking table: **7 mi (11.3 km)**, **450 ft**, Moderate, "No swimming!"
  **Access road CLOSED, likely until Summer 2027**; hikers may park on the CA-190 shoulder and walk in.
- **Telescope Peak** — NPS hiking table: **12.2 mi (19.6 km)**, **3,375 ft (1,028 m)**, **Difficult**.
  Summit **11,049 ft (3,368 m)** — the park's high point. Trailhead at Mahogany Flat (8,200 ft),
  high-clearance/4x4 dirt road, often closed in winter by snow and ice.
- **Wildrose Peak** — **7.9 mi (12.7 km)**, **2,572 ft (784 m)**, **Difficult**; recommended by NPS as a
  summer hike to escape valley heat.
- **Desolation Canyon** — **3.6 mi (5.8 km)**, **600 ft (183 m)**, Moderate to Difficult, rock scrambling.
- **Eureka Dunes** — tallest sand dunes in California, high-clearance access only, free primitive campground.
- **Aguereberry Point** — named by the park FAQ as one of the best sunset spots.
- **Rhyolite ghost town** — just outside the park near Beatty, NV; "the best preserved ghost town in the
  Death Valley area" (park FAQ).

## Dark sky
Sources: https://www.nps.gov/deva/learn/nature/lightscape.htm and
https://www.nps.gov/places/dantes-view.htm — fetched 2026-09-17
- Death Valley is an **International Dark Sky Park classified at the highest (Gold Tier) level** by the
  International Dark-Sky Association.
- Best conditions: far from cities, and an extremely dry climate means few clouds.
- The park runs an annual **Dark Sky Festival** (https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/darkskyfestival.htm);
  recent editions have been held in late February / early March.
  UNVERIFIED: the 2027 Dark Sky Festival dates — not published on the pages fetched 2026-09-17.

## Wildflowers
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/learn/nature/wildflowers.htm — fetched 2026-09-17
- **Current status as posted: "The 2026 superbloom is over. Low-elevation flowers have set seed...
  Higher elevations have scattered blooms."**
- Typical bloom windows: **mid-February to mid-April** at low elevations (alluvial fans and foothills);
  **mid-April to early May** at 3,000–5,000 ft; **early May to mid-July** at 5,000–11,000 ft.
- Superblooms "tend to average once a decade, with the most recent superbloom years being
  **2026, 2016, 2005, and 1998**." A good year needs well-spaced rain through fall/winter/spring,
  warmth, and no drying winds; the best blooms follow an early September–October storm plus an El Niño winter.
- Phacelia: "DO NOT TOUCH- causes itching and rash."
