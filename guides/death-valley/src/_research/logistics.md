# Logistics — Death Valley NP

## Fees
Sources: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/fees.htm and the NPS structured data feed
https://www.nps.gov/deva/structured_data_deva.json — both fetched 2026-09-17
- **Private vehicle: $30, valid 7 days** (all occupants of one non-commercial car/truck/van; unlimited re-entry).
- **Motorcycle: $25 / 7 days.** **Individual on foot or bicycle: $15 / 7 days.**
- **Death Valley Annual Pass: $55** (12 months from purchase).
- **America the Beautiful interagency annual pass: $80** for US residents; **$250** for non-residents.
  Discounted/free versions exist for seniors, military, people with disabilities, and 4th graders.
  (The non-resident tier is new; verify at point of sale.)
- **There are no staffed entrance stations on the park roads.** Ways to pay:
  - **Online in advance at Recreation.gov** (print the pass and display it on the dashboard — this is
    what NPS tells visitors entering on a dirt road to do).
  - **Staffed**: Furnace Creek Visitor Center; Stovepipe Wells Ranger Station; Furnace Creek Campground
    kiosk (October 15 – April 15 only).
  - **24/7 automated fee machines** at: Furnace Creek Visitor Center, Stovepipe Wells, Grapevine Ranger
    Station, Ryan Kiosk, Zabriskie Point, Badwater, Hell's Gate, and various campgrounds.
    **Credit cards only — not digital wallets.**
  - **Authorized vendors outside the park**: Charles Brown Store, Death Valley Marketplace,
    Lone Pine Interagency Visitor Center, Ridgecrest Area Convention & Visitors Bureau.
- **"Death Valley does not accept cash."**
- 2026 fee-free days listed: Feb 16, May 25, Jun 14, Jul 3–5, Aug 25, Sep 17, Oct 27, Nov 11.
- **No timed entry, no reservation is required to enter the park** — the only reservation systems are for
  Furnace Creek Campground (Oct 15–Apr 15) and the designated backcountry roadside sites.

## Getting there
Sources: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/directions.htm, https://www.nps.gov/deva/faqs.htm,
https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/lodging.htm, NPS structured data feed — all fetched 2026-09-17
- **Las Vegas via Pahrump (fastest).** Park FAQ, verbatim: "The fastest route from Las Vegas is through
  Pahrump, Nevada. From Interstate 15 take NV 160 to Pahrump. Just north of town, turn left on Bell Vista
  Road. Take Bell Vista (becomes Stateline Road in California) to Death Valley Junction and turn right on
  CA 127, then a quick left on CA 190 to Death Valley. **The whole trip is about 120 miles and takes 2 hours.**"
  The NPS lodging page separately gives Las Vegas as "approximately a **2.5 hour** drive from the Furnace
  Creek Visitor Center" — that is the conservative figure. CONFLICT noted; guide gives ~2–2.5 hr and 120 mi.
- **Las Vegas via Beatty**: US-95 north to Beatty, NV, then **SR-374** (Daylight Pass) into the park.
  NPS gives **Beatty ≈ 45 minutes from Furnace Creek**. NPS also states **Beatty is 43 miles from
  Furnace Creek** (in the EV-charging note). Total mileage from Las Vegas via Beatty is not given by NPS —
  UNVERIFIED, so the guide does not state one.
- **Pahrump, NV ≈ 1.5 hours** from Furnace Creek Visitor Center.
- **Shoshone, CA ≈ 1 hour** (CA-178 entrance).
- **Lone Pine, CA ≈ 1 hour 45 minutes** — west side, via SR-136 to CA-190 at Olancha.
- **Ridgecrest, CA ≈ 2.5 hours** — via SR-178 (Panamint Valley Rd) from Trona.
- **Bishop, CA ≈ 3.5 hours.**
- Los Angeles: **NPS does not publish a drive time or mileage from Los Angeles.** UNVERIFIED — the guide
  describes the route (I-5/CA-14 north to Ridgecrest, then CA-178 via Trona, or US-395 to Olancha and
  CA-190) and gives Ridgecrest's official 2.5-hour figure as the last leg rather than inventing a total.
- **Paved entrances.** East side: CA-190 from Death Valley Junction; SR-374 from Beatty NV;
  CA-178 from Shoshone CA; SR-267 from US-95 NV. West side: CA-190 from Olancha CA (SR-136 from Lone Pine);
  SR-178 (Panamint Valley Rd) from Trona CA.
  NOTE: the SR-267 / Grapevine Canyon approach from Nevada is **closed until further notice**
  (NPS structured data, Mesquite Spring directions) — this is the Scotty's Castle / Bonnie Clare closure.
- **Nearest major airport: Las Vegas (LAS)** — ~2–2.5 hours to Furnace Creek. There is no public
  transportation to or within the park. Small public airport at Furnace Creek (no fuel, bring tie-downs)
  and a rough paved strip at Stovepipe Wells (no fuel).
- **GPS warning, verbatim:** "DO NOT DEPEND ONLY ON YOUR VEHICLE GPS NAVIGATION SYSTEM. GPS Navigation to
  remote locations like Death Valley National Park are notoriously unreliable. Numerous travelers have been
  directed to the wrong location and dead-end or closed roads." **There is no street address for the park
  or the Furnace Creek Visitor Center**; coordinates are **N 36°27.70, W 116°52.00**. Download the NPS App
  and save Death Valley for offline use.

## Fuel
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/directions.htm — fetched 2026-09-17
- **Three gas stations in the park:**
  - **The Oasis at Death Valley (Furnace Creek)** — two grades of gasoline **and diesel**.
  - **Panamint Springs Resort** — gasoline **and diesel**.
  - **Stovepipe Wells Resort** — **87-octane gasoline only, no diesel**.
- In-park fuel is a captive market and is reliably far more expensive than in gateway towns; NPS does not
  publish prices and neither does the guide. Fill up in Pahrump, Beatty, Lone Pine or Ridgecrest.
  UNVERIFIED as a number — stated only qualitatively.
- Backcountry-roads page: "Top off your gas tank before starting a trip."

## EV charging
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/directions.htm — fetched 2026-09-17 (last updated Jan 24, 2025)
- **"There are no rapid chargers inside the park. The closest rapid chargers are in Beatty, Nevada,
  43 miles from Furnace Creek."** NPS warns it is "unwise to expect to be able to use these to charge
  during peak visitation times."
- **Furnace Creek**: "As of December 2023, the two chargers at The Inn at Death Valley are not working and
  two of the 4 chargers at The Ranch at Death Valley are working and have adapters, instructions available
  at the front desk." Level 2, J1772, 30 A max / 7.2 kW @ 240 VAC. **Best used overnight.**
  CONFLICT: the NPS lodging page (last updated May 11, 2026) says "There are four electric vehicle chargers
  available" at each of The Inn and The Ranch, without repeating the out-of-service note. The guide says
  four are installed at each and that some have been out of service, and tells people to confirm.
- **Stovepipe Wells**: four Blink Series 8 Plus chargers adjacent to the General Store, up to 19.2 kW
  @ 240 VAC per port, J1772.
- **Do NOT plug an EV into the Furnace Creek Campground hookup pedestals** — NPS says this "could
  permanently damage your vehicle and the campground's electrical system. You could be liable."

## Cell service, Wi-Fi and water
Sources: NPS structured data feed; https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/directions.htm;
https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/safety.htm — fetched 2026-09-17
- **"Cellphone access within the park is limited. Visitors with certain major carriers can sometimes find
  service (slow and limited) at Furnace Creek and Stovepipe Wells. Phones typically need to be set to allow
  for roaming, as the cellphone towers are third-party operated."**
- Per-campground note in the feed: "Cell service is very spotty. Not all carriers offer reception in Death
  Valley. **Expect zero connectivity during periods of higher-than-normal visitation** (holidays, weekends,
  special events)." Emigrant Campground's field simply reads "No service."
- Backcountry-roads page: "**cell phone reception is non-existant in most areas of the park.**"
- **Free public Wi-Fi at the Furnace Creek Visitor Center, 24 hrs/day.** Stovepipe Wells Village offers
  complimentary Wi-Fi to its guests and campers. The directions page says "wifi is only available for
  purchase at the Furnace Creek Ranch (Hotel)" — CONFLICT with the feed's free-visitor-center Wi-Fi;
  both are stated, they are different facilities, and the guide reports both.
- **Drinking water** is available at **Furnace Creek, Stovepipe Wells and Panamint Springs** (safety page).
  Wildrose, Thorndike and Mahogany Flat have **no water** — nearest is ~31–39 miles north at Stovepipe Wells.
- Carry at least **one gallon (4 liters) per person per day**; a 5-gallon emergency container is standard
  for backcountry driving. "Springs and other natural water sources may be dry or contaminated."

## Vehicle requirements for dirt roads
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/backcountryroads.htm and
https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/10303151 — fetched 2026-09-17
- **High clearance is defined** (recreation.gov, for the backcountry roadside sites) as "a SUV or truck with
  at least **15 inch tire rims AND at least 8 inches of ground clearance** from the lowest point of the
  frame, body, or suspension."
- Paved-road sights (Badwater, Artists Drive, Zabriskie, Dante's View, the dunes, Harmony Borax) need
  **no special vehicle**. Graded gravel spurs to Devils Golf Course, Natural Bridge, Mosaic Canyon and
  Twenty Mule Team Canyon are "passable by most vehicles"/"typically passable by sedans" but can develop
  washouts and ruts.
- **Titus Canyon**: 2WD high-clearance recommended, 4WD may be needed after weather.
  **Racetrack**: 4x4, high clearance and heavy-duty tires; "standard rental vehicles are not recommended,
  and often get flat tires"; **at least 3.5 hours each way** from Furnace Creek.
- "Most vehicle rental agreements restrict vehicles to paved roads." **Farabee's Jeep Rentals** operates an
  outlet in Furnace Creek with vehicles outfitted for backcountry roads.
- Carry **two full-size spares** for serious backcountry driving, plus plug kit, 12 V compressor, lug wrench
  and a complete jack. Off-road tires, not highway tires.

## Services summary
- **Furnace Creek**: visitor center (8 am–5 pm daily, year-round), The Inn and The Ranch (lodging, dining),
  general store, gas + diesel, showers/laundry for a fee at The Ranch, golf course, post office, airport,
  Farabee's Jeep Rentals, EV chargers, free Wi-Fi at the VC.
- **Stovepipe Wells**: hotel, restaurant and saloon, general store, 24-hour gas station (87 octane only),
  ranger station (hours vary — call 760-786-3200), pool, showers and Wi-Fi for guests/passes, EV chargers,
  concession-run RV park with full hookups.
- **Panamint Springs**: resort lodging, camping/RV, restaurant, gas + diesel. Open all year.
- **Nothing else**. Plan on no services for 50+ miles in most directions.

## Lodging
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/lodging.htm — fetched 2026-09-17 (last updated May 11, 2026);
https://www.oasisatdeathvalley.com and https://www.stovepipedeathvalley.com — fetched 2026-09-17
- Park framing: "Death Valley is a big place - **3.4 million acres**! It is also remote. Driving distances
  between sightseeing and lodging can be long."
- **The Inn at Death Valley** (Furnace Creek, open all year): "privately-owned historic inn provides first
  class resort accommodations." Xanterra: "an elegant hideaway since 1927," AAA Four Diamond for 37+ years;
  guest rooms plus casitas; spring-fed pool, gardens, The Inn Dining Room. Reservations 800-236-7916.
- **The Ranch at Death Valley** (Furnace Creek, open all year): "private motel provides lodging
  accommodations, restaurants, shopping and a gas station." Family-oriented — renovated rooms with private
  patios, cottages, spring-fed pool, town square, The Last Kind Words Saloon, ice cream parlor, and the
  **Furnace Creek Golf Course, the world's lowest at 214 ft below sea level**. Same reservation line.
  Both properties came through a "$200 million renaissance" renovation.
- **Stovepipe Wells Village** (open all year, 760-786-7090 / 833-778-9290): "recently renovated resort
  accommodations, limited recreational vehicle camping with full hookups, a restaurant and saloon featuring
  fresh seasonal fare, a gift shop, and a **24-hour gas station**." Pool (with a lift), two accessible rooms.
  Address 51880 Highway 190.
- **Panamint Springs Resort** (open all year, 775-482-7680): "resort accommodations, camping, a restaurant
  and gas station," on the park's west side on CA-190. Assistance button at the gas station.
  UNVERIFIED: room/cabin/tent-cabin breakdown and RV hookup counts — panamintsprings.com returned a bot
  challenge on 2026-09-17 and could not be read.
- **Gateway towns**, with NPS-published drive times from the Furnace Creek Visitor Center:
  Beatty NV ~45 min · Shoshone CA ~1 hr · Pahrump NV ~1.5 hr · Lone Pine CA ~1 hr 45 min ·
  Ridgecrest CA ~2.5 hr · Las Vegas NV ~2.5 hr · Bishop CA ~3.5 hr.
  Tecopa (hot springs) is near Shoshone; NPS gives no separate drive time — UNVERIFIED.

## Ranger programs
Source: https://www.nps.gov/deva/planyourvisit/ranger-programs.htm — fetched 2026-09-17
- "Programs are typically offered **mid-November to mid-April**." Summer programs are limited by heat.
- Walk-up, no reservation: Badwater talk (45 min), Harmony Borax Works walk (45 min, 1/3 mi paved),
  Golden Canyon hike (1 hr, 3/4 mi), Sand Dunes walk (1 hr), family programs, History of Stovepipe Wells
  (15 min), indoor evening programs, **Night Sky talks** (45 min–1 hr) at Furnace Creek.
- **Paleontology hikes** on select January–March dates (moderately strenuous, 7 mi round trip) —
  reserve via Recreation.gov / 1-877-444-6777, released two weeks ahead at 7:00 am PST, $1 reservation fee.

## In-park drive times used in the itineraries
NPS-published (all fetched 2026-09-17): Furnace Creek VC → Zabriskie Point **15 min**;
→ Badwater Basin **30 min / 17 mi**; → Dante's View **45 min**; → Ubehebe Crater **1 hour**;
→ Emigrant Campground **40 min**. Artists Drive itself takes **25–45 min** for its 9 one-way miles,
entered 8.5 mi south of the Badwater Rd/CA-190 junction. Golden Canyon trailhead is **2 mi** south of
CA-190 on Badwater Road. Stovepipe Wells is **24 mi (39 km)** west of Furnace Creek on CA-190.
UNVERIFIED (derived, not published by NPS): Furnace Creek → Stovepipe Wells ≈ 30 minutes, inferred from
the 24-mile paved distance and the published 40-minute time to Emigrant, 10 miles further on.
The guide states this leg as "24 miles west on CA-190, about half an hour."
