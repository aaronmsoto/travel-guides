What these dates mean

Weather

November averages a 64°F high and 37°F low on the canyon floor, and about ten November nights drop below freezing. Great hiking days; mornings and evenings need layers.

Daylight

About ten hours. Sunrise is a little after 7 a.m. and the canyon is in shadow well before the ~5:15 p.m. sunset, so we start early and eat dinner in town.

Shuttle

The Zion Canyon shuttle runs through Nov 28: first bus 7:00 a.m., last bus into the canyon 5:00 p.m., last bus out 6:15 p.m. Private cars cannot drive the Scenic Drive while it runs. The free Springdale town shuttle runs the same season.

Angels Landing

The Sep–Nov seasonal lottery closed in July, so the route in is the day-before lottery: apply 12:01 a.m.–3 p.m. MT the day before, $6 per application for up to six people, $3 per person if drawn. Scout Lookout needs no permit.

The Narrows

Open unless flow is above 150 cfs or a flash flood warning is issued. Water will be in the low 40s °F — rented dry pants or a drysuit from a Springdale outfitter is the difference between fun and misery.

Crowds

Visitation drops sharply in November. We leave the day before Thanksgiving, just ahead of the holiday surge — but Springdale lodging still books up for that week.

Bring

  • Layers for 35–65°F: warm base layer, fleece or puffy, wind shell, beanie and gloves for mornings
  • Real hiking shoes with grip; microspikes if the forecast turns cold (shaded slickrock ices)
  • Headlamp — the canyon is dark by 5:30 p.m.
  • 2 liters of water per person per hike; there is water at the visitor center and Zion Lodge
  • Recreation.gov account set up in advance for the Angels Landing day-before lottery
  • Narrows day: rent dry pants or drysuit, neoprene socks, canyon boots and a walking stick in Springdale; bring a dry bag
  • America the Beautiful pass if anyone has one ($35 per vehicle otherwise, good 7 days)
  • Snacks and a packed lunch — there is no food in the canyon beyond Zion Lodge

Still deciding

  • Which big hikes people want most — Angels Landing (lottery), The Narrows (cold-water gear), or Observation Point via East Mesa (a drive to the east side)
  • Whether to add a Kolob Canyons detour on the way out (Exit 40 on I-15, about an hour)
  • Wednesday timing — leave by noon to beat Thanksgiving-eve traffic, or make it a full day and drive after dinner

Want to come?

The more the better: hikes are more fun in a group, cars fill up efficiently, and dinners in Springdale are better with company. You do not need to do every day — join for the whole stretch or drive in for a day or two.

  • Since June 7, 2026, large vehicles are banned from the Zion–Mount Carmel Highway between Canyon Junction and the East Entrance — anything over 35 ft 9 in long, 7 ft 10 in wide, 11 ft 4 in tall or 50,000 lbs is turned around at the gate.   • South Campground reopened May 29, 2026 after rehabilitation. Reservations are required and open only 14 days ahead. source

Why go

Zion is a narrow, 2,000-foot-deep gash of Navajo sandstone with a river running through the bottom, and almost everything famous about the park happens inside that one canyon. The two signature hikes go straight at the geology: Angels Landing climbs a fin to a knife-edge strung with chains, and The Narrows abandons dry land and walks you upstream between walls you can touch on both sides.

Know this first: from March 7 to November 28, 2026 you cannot drive Zion Canyon Scenic Drive. A free shuttle runs it every 5–10 minutes, nine stops, no ticket. The rest of the park — the Zion–Mount Carmel Highway, Kolob Canyons, Kolob Terrace Road — stays open to cars all year, and is where you go when the canyon feels full.

When to go

Best

Spring

Mar–May

Highs 66–86°F · Lows 39–54°F

The sweet spot for temperature and the worst stretch for the Narrows: snowmelt pushes the river past the 150 cfs closure line, and NPS warns it "may be closed for an extended period of time." Nights stay cold — the daily swing can be 32°F or more — and March and April are wet. Crowds arrive fast: March draws almost three times February's visitors, the shuttle goes full-time March 7, 2026, and South Campground reopens.
Hard

Summer

Jun–Aug

Highs 95–100°F · Lows 63–70°F

Hot and crowded — "regularly over 100°F," 16 days above 100°F in an average July, roughly half a million visitors a month, parking often full by 9:00 am. The upside is the Narrows: flow drops to wadeable and water reaches 56–68°F. The downside is the monsoon, July–September, with 14–15 thunderstorm days a month and real flash-flood risk. Hike at dawn and carry a gallon per person.
Best

Fall

Sep–Nov

Highs 64–91°F · Lows 37–61°F

The other sweet spot, and better than spring if the Narrows is your reason for coming — flow is low and the water still tolerable in September. September stays busy (11% of the year's visitors) before visitation drops sharply in November. Daily swings of 30°F are normal, and color shows at the highest elevations by late October. The shuttle's last run drops to 5:00 pm from October 25.
Good

Winter

Dec–Feb

Highs 54–59°F · Lows 30–34°F

The quiet season, and the only time you can drive the Scenic Drive yourself — the shuttle stops after November 28 and returns only December 26 to January 2. Expect 13–20 freezing nights a month and snow that "typically disappears within a matter of hours at lower elevations." Ice on the Angels Landing chains and the Riverside Walk is the real hazard; Kolob Terrace Road and often Kolob Canyons Road close with snow.

Angels Landing

HikeStrenuousPermit lottery
Distance
1.9 mi each way to Scout Lookout, plus 0.5 mi each way on the chains (~4.8 mi round trip)
Elevation gain
1,187 ft to Scout Lookout
Time
3–4 hr to Scout Lookout; 1–2 hr more for the chains
Start / parking
The Grotto (shuttle stop 6)
Best time
First shuttle at 7:00 am — the switchbacks face the sun and the chains bottleneck by mid-morning
The park's signature hike, and one of the most exposed maintained trails in the national park system. The West Rim Trail climbs the canyon wall in paved switchbacks, cools off in Refrigerator Canyon, then stacks 21 tight switchbacks — Walter's Wiggles — to Scout Lookout. From there a half-mile fin runs to the summit along a ridge under three feet wide in places, chains bolted into the rock, drop-offs of up to 1,000 feet.

Permit lottery. Required for everyone past Scout Lookout, via recreation.gov only. $6 per application (up to six people) plus $3 per person if awarded. Apply in the seasonal lottery months ahead, or in the day-before lottery — 12:01 a.m. to 3 p.m. MT, results at 4 p.m. MT. Start time is measured at The Grotto. No permit is needed to reach Scout Lookout.

Tips from the research
  • Hike to Scout Lookout without a permit — great view, and you decide about the chains there
  • Skip the chains if the rock is wet or icy or the wind is up; there is no easy retreat mid-ridge
  • Fill up at The Grotto — no water past it, and restrooms end at Scout Lookout

Season note: Chains and slickrock ice up in winter; the route stays open with a permit, but traction devices matter

Accessibility: Not accessible. Unpaved, rocky, under 3 ft wide in places; not for young children. Pets prohibited

Sources: nps.gov · nps.gov

The Narrows (bottom-up)

CanyonStrenuousNo permit
Distance
Up to a 10 mi round trip to Big Spring; turn around anywhere
Elevation gain
Negligible — you walk upstream in the riverbed
Time
2 hr for a taste; all day to Big Spring
Start / parking
Temple of Sinawava (shuttle stop 9), then 1 mi on the Riverside Walk
Best time
Late summer and early fall, when flow is low and the water is warmest
There is no trail — the Virgin River is the route. You wade, sometimes thigh-deep, up a slot where the walls close to a couple of dozen feet apart and rise a thousand. No permit is needed, which is why it is also the most crowded hike in the park. Most people go an hour or two upstream and turn back; Big Spring is a ten-mile round trip and a full day.
Tips from the research
  • Check flow and the flash flood rating that morning — 150 cfs closes it
  • Rent canyoneering boots, neoprene socks and a stick in Springdale
  • 56–68°F in summer but 41–53°F spring and fall; dress for hypothermia, not a swim

Season note: Often closed for weeks during spring snowmelt; closes above 150 cfs and in any Flash Flood Warning

Accessibility: The paved Riverside Walk to the river's edge is accessible; the Narrows is not

Sources: nps.gov · nps.gov

Riverside Walk

HikeEasyNo permit
Distance
1 mi each way
Elevation gain
154 ft
Time
1–2 hr
Start / parking
Temple of Sinawava (shuttle stop 9)
Best time
Early morning or the last shuttle up — the walls glow and the crowd thins
The paved path at the end of the shuttle line, following the river upstream while the canyon squeezes shut. Hanging gardens seep from the walls, and the trail ends where the river fills the canyon floor — the doorway to the Narrows. Nearly everyone who rides the shuttle walks it, and it earns the traffic.
Tips from the research
  • Ride to the last stop first thing; queues here are worst mid-day
  • Stop where the pavement ends even if you skip the Narrows — that is the view
  • Restrooms and water are at the trailhead and nowhere beyond

Season note: Can close in winter when the pavement ices over

Accessibility: Concrete, typically 77 in wide at about a 4.6% grade — wheelchair and stroller friendly, with some irregular paving and minor drop-offs

Emerald Pools

HikeModerateNo permit
Distance
2.2 mi round trip to the Middle pools; Upper adds 0.3 mi each way
Elevation gain
333 ft to Middle; 76 ft more to Upper
Time
1–2 hr
Start / parking
Emerald Pools Trailhead across from Zion Lodge (shuttle stop 5), or The Grotto (stop 6) via the Kayenta Trail
Best time
Spring, when the ephemeral waterfalls are actually falling
The classic family hike from Zion Lodge, and three destinations you can take as far as you like. Lower runs under a dripping overhang behind a thin waterfall; Middle climbs a sandstone ledge to open canyon views; Upper ends at a green pool in a dead-end amphitheater below the Great White Throne.
Tips from the research
  • Up the Kayenta Trail from The Grotto, down to Zion Lodge, and it becomes a loop
  • Do not enter the pools — fragile wildlife water, and swimming is prohibited
  • The pools are on the cyanobacteria advisory list; keep dogs and children out

Season note: Open year-round; the waterfalls are seasonal and can be dry by late summer

Accessibility: Lower is partly paved but rough; Middle and Upper are unpaved with steps and ledges

Sources: nps.gov

Canyon Overlook Trail

HikeModerateNo permit
Distance
1 mi round trip
Elevation gain
154 ft gain, 49 ft loss
Time
1–2 hr
Start / parking
Small lot on SR-9 immediately east of the Zion–Mount Carmel Tunnel — drive yourself, no shuttle

The best view in Zion per unit of effort. A half-mile of sandstone steps, handrails and a catwalk bolted over a dark slot canyon opens onto red and cream slickrock, ending at a fenced cliff edge above the switchbacks of the Zion–Mount Carmel Highway, with the Towers of the Virgin beyond.

Accessibility: Not accessible — steps, uneven rock, tread narrowing to 12 in, drop-offs. Pets prohibited

Zion Canyon Scenic Drive & Court of the Patriarchs

Scenic driveEasyNo permit
Distance
Nine shuttle stops end to end
Elevation gain
None
Time
About 45 min one way; about 1.5 hr for a full round trip without getting off
Start / parking
Zion Canyon Visitor Center (shuttle stop 1)
Best time
The first shuttle at 7:00 am, or the last runs of the evening for low light
The spine of the park, and what almost every visitor actually does. The shuttle runs the canyon floor past the Court of the Patriarchs, Zion Lodge, The Grotto, Big Bend and the Temple of Sinawava, walls rising 2,000 feet on both sides. Court of the Patriarchs at stop 4 is the set-piece: a short steep walk to an overlook facing Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, named in 1916.
Tips from the research
  • Shuttles come every 5–10 minutes — get off freely, you are never stranded long
  • Ride to stop 9 first, then work back down; the crowd moves the other way
  • Court of the Patriarchs faces east: brilliant in the morning, flat by afternoon

Season note: Drivable in your own car only outside shuttle season, and it closes for the day when its parking fills — common in December, January and February

Accessibility: Every shuttle bus is wheelchair accessible (lift limit 600 lbs; chairs to 45 in long by 25 in wide). The Court of the Patriarchs overlook walk is short but steep

Sources: nps.gov · nps.gov

Zion–Mount Carmel Highway & Checkerboard Mesa

Scenic driveEasyNo permit
Distance
10.7 mi between Canyon Junction and the East Entrance
Elevation gain
Climbs out of the canyon in a series of engineered switchbacks
Time
45 min to 1.5 hr with stops
Start / parking
Canyon Junction (SR-9) — open to private vehicles year-round
Best time
Late afternoon heading east, when the slickrock warms up
Opened July 3, 1930, this is the road that made the Grand Circle tour of Zion, Bryce and the Grand Canyon possible: switchbacks stacked up the canyon wall, a long tunnel bored through the cliff, and a different landscape beyond. East of the tunnel the red canyon gives way to cream slickrock domes, ending at Checkerboard Mesa, cross-hatched into a giant grid.
Tips from the research
  • Pull into every signed overlook; Checkerboard Mesa has its own near the East Entrance
  • The drive to do when shuttle queues are absurd — it needs no shuttle
  • Bighorn sheep are common here; slow down and do not approach

Season note: Open all year; snow and ice are possible on the switchbacks in winter

Accessibility: Fully drivable; the overlooks are roadside pullouts with short, mostly level approaches

Sources: nps.gov · nps.gov

Distance

Elevation gain
152 ft

30 min–2 hr

Zion Canyon Visitor Center (shuttle stop 1) or Canyon Junction (stop 3)

"Pa'rus" is Paiute for bubbling water, and the trail follows the river from the visitor center to Canyon Junction on pavement and boardwalk, with the best straight-on views of the Watchman in the park. It is Zion's only pet-friendly trail and its only bike trail, which makes it far more useful than its easy rating suggests.
Tips from the research
  • The one trail where leashed dogs are allowed — leash no longer than 6 feet
  • Bikes allowed, so it is the natural link from Springdale to Canyon Junction
  • Sunset over the Watchman from the bridges is the classic Zion photograph

Season note: Open and usable year-round, including when the shuttle is not running

Accessibility: 93.6% paved with the remainder hardwood decking, minimum tread width 96 inches, typical grade 2.6% — wheelchair accessible

Observation Point via East Mesa Trail

ViewpointModerateNo permit
Distance
6.8 mi round trip
Elevation gain
300 ft
Time
About 3 hr round trip
Start / parking
East Mesa Trailhead, reached only by high-clearance or 4WD dirt roads off North Fork Road
Best time
Morning, before the mesa bakes and before afternoon storms build
Observation Point sits roughly 700 feet above Angels Landing and looks down on it, and the East Mesa Trail gets you there almost level — you start high on the plateau and walk to the rim instead of climbing out of the canyon. The catch is access: a rough dirt road, no shuttle, no services. The best big overlook in Zion per unit of suffering, and no lottery.
Tips from the research
  • Check the road first — a passenger car is not enough, and mud shuts it for days
  • Park only in designated spaces; illegally parked vehicles are cited or towed
  • Carry all your water; there is nothing at this trailhead

Season note: Roads can be "extremely muddy and inaccessible in the winter, spring, and after rainstorms"

Accessibility: Not accessible; the trailhead is not reachable by ordinary vehicles

  • Kolob Canyons & Timber Creek Overlook — A separate entrance at I-15 Exit 40, 40 miles from Zion Canyon and 17 miles south of Cedar City: a five-mile scenic drive under 2,000-foot crimson walls, no shuttle at all. Getting around

From March 7 to November 28, 2026, and again December 26 to January 2, personal vehicles are barred from Zion Canyon Scenic Drive. The free Zion Canyon Line runs it with nine stops — Visitor Center, Human History Museum, Canyon Junction, Court of the Patriarchs, Zion Lodge, The Grotto, Weeping Rock, Big Bend, Temple of Sinawava — every 5–10 minutes, about 45 minutes end to end. Only Zion Lodge and Canyon Trail Rides guests with reservations, and medical accommodation permit holders, may drive it.

The free Springdale Line runs nine stops through town every 10–15 minutes to the park's Pedestrian/Bike Entrance. Park in town and ride in: the visitor center lot holds just over 350 cars and NPS says parking "often fills by early or mid-morning" February through November. Everything east of Canyon Junction, plus Kolob Canyons and Kolob Terrace Road, stays open to cars year-round.

Entrance fees & passes

$35 per private vehicle, $30 per motorcycle, $20 per person on foot or by bicycle (16+); standard passes are valid 7 days. A Zion Annual Pass is $70. America the Beautiful is $80 for residents and $250 for non-residents; Senior Annual $20, Senior Lifetime $80, Military and Access passes free. No charge for either shuttle. 2026 fee-free days: Feb 16, May 25, Jun 14, Jul 3–5, Aug 25, Sep 17, Oct 27, Nov 11.

Reservations & timed entry

You do not need a reservation or timed entry to enter Zion, and you do not need one to ride the shuttle — NPS states both plainly. The only things you reserve are an Angels Landing permit, a wilderness or canyoneering permit, a campsite, or a room. Camping is the tight one: Watchman books six months out and the park says campgrounds are "full almost every night mid-March through late November."

Fuel, food, water, cell

Open 24 hours a day, year-round. Zion Canyon Visitor Center 8 am–5 pm, Human History Museum 10 am–5 pm, Kolob Canyons Visitor Center 8 am–12 pm and 1 pm–5 pm; rangers answer 435-772-3256 from 10 am to 5 pm MT. Zion Lodge is the only place inside the park to buy food, and holds the gift shop and post office. Water and restrooms are at the visitor center, The Grotto, Scout Lookout and the Riverside Walk trailhead. Groceries, fuel, restaurants and the outfitters renting Narrows gear are in Springdale. Cell coverage is unreliable in the canyon — download maps first.

Permits

Required

Angels Landing permit

Everyone hiking past Scout Lookout needs one, via recreation.gov only. A seasonal lottery runs months ahead; a day-before lottery opens 12:01 a.m. and closes 3 p.m. MT with results at 4 p.m. MT. Cost: $6 per application (up to six people, non-refundable) plus $3 per person if awarded, refundable if you cancel two or more days out on a seasonal permit. Your start time is measured at The Grotto and rangers check anywhere on the trail. The 2026 seasonal lotteries ran Feb 13–25, Apr 1–20 and Jul 1–20; the one for Dec 1, 2026 – Feb 28, 2027 opens October 1, 2026.

Official page

Optional / special

The Narrows bottom-up

No permit. You can walk upstream from the Temple of Sinawava as far as Big Spring without one. The limits are the river, not paperwork: the route closes above 150 cfs and during Flash Flood Warnings.

Official page

Required

The Narrows top-down / overnight

The 16-mile through-hike from Chamberlain's Ranch needs a wilderness permit, as does any overnight campsite in the Narrows. Three routes to a day permit: an advance reservation on recreation.gov at least a day ahead, a daily lottery two days before (12:00 a.m.–3:00 p.m. MT), or first-come, first-served at the Wilderness Desk the day before. Day use is $6 per application plus $10 per person per day; overnight is $20 per reservation plus $7 per person per night. Arrange your own ride to Chamberlain's Ranch.

Official page

Required

Canyoneering permits

Required for every canyon needing ropes or descending gear. Advance reservations open up to three months ahead and stay bookable until 5:00 p.m. MT the day before; a daily lottery runs two days out (12:00 a.m.–3:00 p.m. MT), and walk-in spaces go the day before at the Wilderness Desk. Fees: $6 per application plus $10 per person.

Official page

Required

Wilderness / backpacking permit

Needed for all overnight backpacking, climbing bivouacs, Narrows and tributary through-hikes, and all trips into the Left Fork of North Creek. $20 per reservation plus $7 per person per night. Permits are picked up in person by the reservation holder at the Visitor Center Wilderness Desk and are non-transferable. Campfires are prohibited in the Zion Wilderness.

How to book: 100% reservation — no first-come, first-served sites. Lodging

town$$–$$$$

Springdale, UT

At the South Entrance — the park boundary is the north end of town

A single main street running up to the park gate, and where the overwhelming majority of Zion visitors sleep — several dozen hotels, motels, inns and B&Bs in a town of barely 600 people. The decisive feature is the free town shuttle: nine stops the length of town, every 10–15 minutes, same season as the park shuttle, ending at the park's Pedestrian/Bike Entrance. Stay anywhere in town and you reach the park without moving your car. Springdale also has the groceries, restaurants, fuel and the outfitters renting Narrows gear; town parking is paid, with large-vehicle spaces.

Examples: Cliffrose Springdale, Desert Pearl Inn, Cable Mountain Lodge, Flanigan's Inn, Bumbleberry Inn, Hotel DeNovo

in-park lodge$$$–$$$$

Zion Lodge

Shuttle stop 5, in the middle of Zion Canyon

The only lodging inside the park: 76 hotel rooms, 6 suites and 40 historic cabins on the canyon floor at shuttle stop 5, with the Emerald Pools and Kayenta trailheads across the road. It is also the only place inside the park to buy food — Red Rock Grill year-round plus a seasonal café — and holds the gift shop and post office. Guests with reservations are among the few allowed to drive the Scenic Drive in shuttle season. Rooms open roughly a year ahead and popular dates vanish immediately: zionlodge.com.

Examples: Historic cabins with gas fireplaces, Hotel rooms with canyon-facing balconies, Red Rock Grill

Nearby towns: Hurricane, La Verkin, St. George, Cedar City, Kanab

Hurricane and La Verkin on the UT-9 approach; St. George 49 mi; Cedar City 60 mi; Kanab east of the park

Hurricane and La Verkin are the standard budget answer — chain motels, RV parks and supermarkets directly on the UT-9 approach, so you are not detouring, just sleeping earlier on the same road. St. George (49 mi) has the closest airport and the deepest hotel supply; Cedar City (60 mi) is the practical base if you want Kolob Canyons first, 17 miles south at I-15 Exit 40. Kanab is the east-side base for a loop with Bryce Canyon and the North Rim — but check your vehicle size, since June 7, 2026 large vehicles cannot use that route. The tradeoff everywhere is arriving with the morning traffic rather than walking in ahead of it.

Safety & know-before-you-go

The handful of things that actually hurt people here, with the specific rule that prevents each one.

critical

Heat

July averages 100°F with 16 days over 100°F, and June and August are barely cooler. NPS is specific: "Carry enough water, at least one gallon per person per day, and drink it" — and eat with it, because water alone does not replace salt. Start at first light and be off exposed rock by late morning. Heat exhaustion looks like nausea, headache, cramps and cool clammy skin: stop, find shade, elevate the feet, take fluids and food. Heat stroke is an emergency.

critical

Flash floods

Flash floods happen year-round and peak with the monsoon — July and August average 14 and 15 thunderstorm days. They arrive as sudden surges carrying tree trunks and boulders, and a storm miles away can trigger one under blue sky. The park posts a daily flash flood potential rating: not expected, possible, probable, expected. Check it and the river flow before any slot canyon or the Narrows, which closes above 150 cfs and in any Flash Flood Warning. If water rises or muddies, get high — do not run downstream.

critical

Falls

NPS states it directly: "Falls from cliffs on trails have resulted in death." Angels Landing is the sharpest example — press tallies compiled in 2021 counted 13 deaths in falls on Angels Landing and its approach since 2000, most on the final chained section; the park publishes no running total. The mechanics are ordinary: loose sand on slickrock is slippery, the ridge narrows to under three feet with 1,000-foot drops, and two-way traffic forces passing in bad places.

critical

Cold water and hypothermia in the Narrows

The Narrows is a cold-water hike even in July: 56–68°F in summer, 41–53°F spring and fall, 38–45°F in winter, and you are in it for hours with little sun. NPS calls hypothermia "the number one killer of outdoor recreationists, even in summer" and a greater hazard in narrow canyons. Wear quick-drying layers, never cotton; rent neoprene socks and canyoneering boots spring and fall, a wetsuit or drysuit in winter. Carry a stick — the riverbed is slick, uneven and invisible.

important

Toxic cyanobacteria in the Virgin River

A health watch advisory is in effect (updated 2026-05-21) for the North Fork of the Virgin River — the Narrows, Pine Creek, Deep Creek, Kolob Creek, Orderville Canyon, the Emerald Pools — plus North Creek and La Verkin Creek. Do not touch or ingest algae mats, do not submerge your head, and do not drink in-stream water: no recreational filter removes cyanotoxins. Children and dogs are most at risk. Wading is not prohibited.

important

Rockfall

Rockfall is an ongoing hazard throughout Zion, especially near cliff faces — the 2019 rockfall near Weeping Rock still has the East Rim Trail partially closed and Hidden Canyon shut. Do not linger under overhangs or at the base of cliffs, and never throw or roll rocks. If rockfall starts and you cannot move clear, shelter behind a large boulder and pull your pack over your head. Keep your distance from wildlife too: NPS warns that contact with animals or their droppings can transmit dangerous illnesses.

note

Winter ice

Zion Canyon gets 13–20 freezing nights a month December through February, and nearly half the year's precipitation falls December through March. Canyon-floor snow melts fast, but ice persists on shaded slickrock, on the Angels Landing chains, and on the paved Riverside Walk, which can close when it glazes over. Traction devices are worth the space.

Itineraries & my trip

My trip

1 day

One perfect day

  1. 6:15 amPark in Springdale or at the visitor center and be at the stop before the first bus at 7:00 am. In summer the lot is often full by 9.
  2. 7:00 amRide to Temple of Sinawava (stop 9), about 45 minutes end to end. You arrive with the canyon almost to yourself.
  3. 7:45 amWalk the Riverside Walk, 1 mile of pavement to where the canyon closes in. With boots and a stick, continue upstream into The Narrows for an hour or two — check flow and the flash flood rating first.
  4. 10:30 amBack to Zion Lodge (stop 5) for the Emerald Pools: 2.2 miles and 333 feet to Middle, 0.3 miles each way more to Upper. Return via the Kayenta Trail for a loop, and eat at the lodge — the only food in the park.
  5. 1:30 pmTen-minute stops at Court of the Patriarchs (stop 4) and Big Bend (stop 8), then shuttle out and collect the car.
  6. 3:00 pmDrive the Zion–Mount Carmel Highway east through the tunnel into white slickrock domes, ending at Checkerboard Mesa.
  7. 4:30 pmHike Canyon Overlook, 1 mile round trip from the small lot east of the tunnel. Parking is extremely limited; late afternoon gives the best odds and the best light.
  8. 7:00 pmDinner in Springdale, then the Pa'rus Trail for sunset on the Watchman and, once dark, the stars. Paved, flat, no shuttle needed.

2 days

Two days

  1. Day 1 · 6:15 amBe on the 7:00 am first shuttle to The Grotto (stop 6). With an Angels Landing permit this is the morning — the chains jam by mid-morning. Without one, take the same West Rim Trail to Scout Lookout: 1.9 miles and 1,187 feet each way, no permit, most of the view.
  2. Day 1 · 11:30 amRefill at The Grotto and take the Kayenta Trail across to the Emerald Pools, finishing at Zion Lodge for a late lunch.
  3. Day 1 · 2:30 pmShuttle down, stopping at Court of the Patriarchs and the Human History Museum for the park film and the Towers of the Virgin.
  4. Day 1 · 5:00 pmWatchman Trail straight from the visitor center — 3.3 miles, 479 feet, about 90 minutes — for sunset over lower Zion Canyon. No shuttle required.
  5. Day 2 · 7:00 amFirst shuttle to Temple of Sinawava, then the Riverside Walk and The Narrows properly: four to six hours upstream and back gets you well past Wall Street, and starting early buys quiet water and time to spare.
  6. Day 2 · 1:30 pmDry off and eat in Springdale, then pick up the car. The afternoon belongs to the east side.
  7. Day 2 · 3:30 pmDrive the Zion–Mount Carmel Highway out to Checkerboard Mesa and back, stopping at the pullouts. Watch for bighorn sheep.
  8. Day 2 · 5:00 pmCanyon Overlook Trail at golden hour — 1 mile round trip for the view onto the highway switchbacks and lower Zion Canyon.
  9. Day 2 · eveningWith a third morning, leave the canyon: Kolob Canyons off I-15 Exit 40, five-mile scenic drive plus the Timber Creek Overlook Trail. No shuttle, rarely crowded.

Frequently asked

Do I need a reservation to enter Zion?
No. NPS states it plainly: "You do not need a permit or reservation to enter the park" and "You do not need a permit or reservation to ride a park shuttle." You do pay the entrance fee — $35 per vehicle, valid 7 days. The only reservations in play are Angels Landing permits, wilderness and canyoneering permits, campsites and rooms.
Can I drive Zion Canyon Scenic Drive?
Not during shuttle season. In 2026 the shuttle runs March 7 through November 28 plus December 26 to January 2, and personal vehicles are barred throughout; the exceptions are Zion Lodge and Canyon Trail Rides guests with reservations and medical accommodation permit holders. Outside those dates you can drive it — though the road closes for the day once its parking fills, common December through February. Everything east of Canyon Junction, plus Kolob Canyons and Kolob Terrace Road, is drivable all year.
How do I get an Angels Landing permit?
Only through recreation.gov, in one of two lotteries. The seasonal lottery runs months ahead for a three-month block; the one covering December 1, 2026 to February 28, 2027 opens October 1, 2026, closes October 20, permits issued October 25. Otherwise the day-before lottery opens 12:01 a.m. and closes 3 p.m. MT, results at 4 p.m. MT the same day. Either way: $6 per application for up to six people plus $3 per person if you win. No permit is needed to reach Scout Lookout, which is most of the climb and much of the view.
Can kids do the Narrows?
No minimum age, and plenty of families do the first mile or two — but be honest about conditions: you are wading a moving river over slick, uneven, invisible cobbles in water that is 56–68°F at its warmest and 41–53°F spring and fall, and current knee-deep on an adult is waist-deep on a child. Rent proper boots and a stick, go in late summer or early fall when flow is lowest, turn around early, and keep heads out of the water. If in doubt, the Riverside Walk gets the same scenery with none of it.
When is the shuttle running?
In 2026, March 7 to November 28, then a holiday run December 26 to January 2. The first bus leaves the visitor center at 7:00 am all season (8:00 am over the holidays). Last departure up-canyon is 6:00 pm Mar 7–May 16, 7:00 pm May 17–Sep 12, 6:00 pm Sep 13–Oct 24 and 5:00 pm Oct 25–Nov 28; the last bus down from Temple of Sinawava runs about 75 minutes later. Every 5–10 minutes in the canyon, 10–15 in Springdale. Free — no ticket, no reservation.
Is Zion open in winter?
Yes — open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, and the only time you can drive the Scenic Drive yourself. Highs run 54–59°F with 13–20 freezing nights a month, and canyon-floor snow usually melts within hours. The catches: ice on the chains and shaded slickrock, Riverside Walk closures, Kolob Terrace Road closed and Kolob Canyons Road frequently closed, South Campground and Lava Point shut, and the Scenic Drive closing for the day when its parking fills. Watchman stays open year-round.
Where do I park?
The visitor center lot has just over 350 spaces and the Human History Museum 51, and NPS says parking "often fills by early or mid-morning" February through November — in summer often by 9:00 am. The reliable move is to park in Springdale, which has paid street and lot parking including large-vehicle spaces, and ride the free town shuttle to the Pedestrian Entrance. If you must park inside, arrive before 8 am or after 4 pm, and prefer weekdays.
