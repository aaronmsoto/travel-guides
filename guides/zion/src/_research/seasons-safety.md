## Seasonal character
Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/seasons.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Spring: "Daytime temperatures are warming but nights can still be cold. The daily temperature swing can be
  32°F / 0°C or more." Snowmelt raises river levels and "the Narrows may be closed for an extended period of time."
  March sees "almost three times as many visitors as in February." Shuttle returns to full-time use; South Campground opens.
- Summer: "Summer is hot with temperatures regularly over 100°F / 38°C." Monsoon July–September brings flash flood risk.
  "around a half million other people each month"; "All available parking in Zion is often full by 9:00 am."
- Fall: "High and low temperatures can vary by 30°F each day." Fall colors show at the highest elevations by late
  October / early November. Visitation slows, "dropping more dramatically in November." Human History Museum closes
  after Thanksgiving; shuttle runs less frequently.
- Winter: "Temperatures can range from highs of 50-60°F during the day to lows well below freezing."
  "Nearly half of the annual precipitation in Zion Canyon falls between the months of December and March."
  Snow "typically disappears within a matter of hours at lower elevations." Kolob Terrace Road closes;
  South Campground closes; shuttle use limited.

## Visitation
- "65% of visitors come during the busiest 6 months (April through September)." Busiest months by 2019–2023 average:
  June 12% (556,426), July 12% (554,096), September 11% (486,413).
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/visitation.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Nearly 5 million recreation visits per year (2024 and 2025); among the most-visited national parks.
  — NPS visitation search results, 2026-09-17. Guide states "nearly 5 million a year" — FLAGGED as approximate.

## Safety
Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/safety.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Heat: "Carry enough water, at least one gallon per person per day, and drink it."
  Heat exhaustion: nausea, vomiting, fatigue, headache, pale appearance, cramps, cool clammy skin. Stop hiking, get
  shade, rest with feet elevated, take fluids AND food; seek help if symptoms persist beyond two hours.
  Heat stroke: cool immediately "using any available means" and call for emergency help.
- Falls: **"Falls from cliffs on trails have resulted in death."** Loose sand and pebbles are slippery.
  Never throw or roll rocks; stay on trails and back from edges.
- Flash floods: occur year-round in Zion and the desert southwest; sudden increases in water depth/speed carrying
  tree trunks and boulders.
- Rockfall: hazards throughout the park, especially near cliff faces. If you cannot move away, shelter behind a
  large boulder and pull your pack over your head.
- Wildlife: keep distance from animals and their waste; contact can transmit "dangerous illnesses".
- The page mentions "hundreds of incidents" a year involving illness/injury but gives no numeric fatality counts.

### Flash flood potential rating system (used by the park daily)
NOT EXPECTED / POSSIBLE ("Some slot canyons may experience flash flooding") /
PROBABLE ("Some slot canyons are expected to experience flash flooding") /
EXPECTED ("Many slot canyons are expected to experience flash flooding")
— https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/narrowssafety.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Monsoon/thunderstorm months from the climate chart: July (14 thunderstorm days) and August (15) are the peak.

### The Narrows specifically
- Park closes the Narrows when the Virgin River exceeds **150 cfs** and during Flash Flood Warnings.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Water temperatures: summer 56–68°F; spring/fall 41–53°F; winter 38–45°F.
- "Hypothermia ... is the number one killer of outdoor recreationists, even in summer" and is "a greater hazard in
  narrow canyons." — narrowssafety.htm

### Angels Landing falls — stated plainly
- NPS confirms deaths from falls on park trails generally ("Falls from cliffs on trails have resulted in death") but
  publishes no running Angels Landing fatality count.
- **UNVERIFIED against NPS:** press tallies (Salt Lake Tribune / FOX 13 / AP, March 2021) counted **13 deaths from
  falls on Angels Landing or its approach trail since 2000**; an NPS representative was quoted saying 11 of those
  occurred on the final chained section. Later third-party tallies run higher (17–19). The guide uses the 2021
  press figure with attribution and no sensational framing. FLAGGED as not NPS-published.
  Sources: https://www.sltrib.com/news/nation-world/2021/03/21/people-have-died-since/

### Toxic cyanobacteria — CURRENT ADVISORY
Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/toxic-cyanobacteria-bloom-in-the-virgin-river-and-the-streams-of-zion-national-park.htm
(fetched 2026-09-17)
- **Current level: "Health Watch - Check First!" — page last updated May 21, 2026.**
- Affected: North Fork of the Virgin River (The Narrows, Pine Creek, Deep Creek, Kolob Creek, Orderville Canyon,
  Emerald Pools), North Creek / The Subway, La Verkin Creek and tributaries.
- "Do not touch or ingest algae mats." "Do not drink from in stream water in the park."
- "Young children and dogs are especially at risk of exposure." Do not let dogs drink from the rivers.
- "There are no known recreational filtration or disinfection method that can remove cyanotoxins from the water so
  that it is safe for drinking."
- Filtering from springs without visible algae mats is permitted in wilderness areas only.
- The conditions page repeats: "Do not submerge your head in or drink instream water in Zion National Park."
- The advisory does NOT close the Narrows or prohibit wading.
- The cyanotoxin involved is anatoxin-a, first detected July 2020 by Utah DEQ/DWQ and NPS.

### Winter ice
- Angels Landing / chains and Riverside Walk ice up; NPS notes winter closures of Riverside Walk are possible due to
  icy conditions, and the Watchman Trail can be muddy or icy in winter.
  — nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-riverside-walk.htm, hike-watchman-trail.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- UNVERIFIED: the park does not publish a blanket "Angels Landing closes in winter" rule; it stays open with a permit
  and microspikes are a visitor-side recommendation, not an NPS requirement. Guide words it that way.
