# Zion — Logistics: fees, driving, parking, tunnel, services

All facts fetched live on **2026-09-17** unless marked UNVERIFIED.

## Entrance fees and passes
Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/fees.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Private vehicle **$35.00**; motorcycle **$30.00**; per person on foot or bicycle (16+) **$20.00**.
- "Standard passes are valid for 7 days."
- Zion Annual Pass **$70.00**.
- America the Beautiful Annual: **resident $80.00**, **non-resident $250.00**.
- Senior Annual (62+) $20; Senior Lifetime $80; Military Annual and Lifetime free; Access Pass (permanent disability) free.
- 2026 fee-free days: Presidents Day Feb 16; Memorial Day May 25; Flag Day Jun 14; Independence Day weekend Jul 3–5;
  NPS Birthday Aug 25; Constitution Day Sep 17; Theodore Roosevelt's birthday Oct 27; Veterans Day Nov 11.
  (Note: today, 2026-09-17, is Constitution Day — a fee-free day.)
- "All park visitors are required to purchase a recreational use pass upon entering Zion National Park."
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm

## Getting there
Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/directions.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Harry Reid International (LAS), Las Vegas — **170 miles**.
- St. George Regional (SGU), Utah — **49 miles**.
- Cedar City Regional (CDC), Utah — **60 miles**.
- Salt Lake City International (SLC) — **300 miles**.
- Mailing address / South Entrance area: "Zion National Park, 1 Zion Park Blvd., Springdale, UT 84767".
  South Entrance is "located on the north end of Springdale".
- East Entrance: "13 miles west of Mt. Carmel Junction" via State Route 9.
- Kolob Canyons: "Exit 40 on Interstate 15. This is 40 miles from Zion Canyon and 17 miles south of Cedar City."
- To Bryce Canyon from the east side via Highway 20 north of Cedar City: "roughly 150 miles long, taking 2.5 hours."
- UNVERIFIED: NPS does not publish drive TIMES from Las Vegas or Salt Lake City. The guide gives the official
  mileages and describes the route (I-15 N to UT-9 E via exit 16/Hurricane, or I-15 to UT-17 to UT-9) without
  inventing durations, except the one NPS-published 2.5 h figure for the Bryce route. FLAGGED.

## Parking
Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/traffic.htm (fetched 2026-09-17), plus directions.htm
- Zion Canyon Visitor Center lot: **over 350 spaces** — the park's largest. Zion Human History Museum: **51 spaces**.
  Nature Center: two lots.
- "Parking often fills by early or mid-morning" February through November. Seasons page: "All available parking in
  Zion is often full by 9:00 am."
- Busiest: Easter week, Memorial Day weekend, July 4, Pioneer Day (July 24), Labor Day weekend.
- "Zion Canyon Scenic Drive will close for the day when there are no more legal parking spaces – often in December,
  January, and February."
- Springdale "offers parking for a fee" on streets and in lots, including large-vehicle parking, with the free town
  shuttle to the Pedestrian Entrance.
- SunTran bus service connects regional communities to the park.
- Advice from the park: arrive early or late in the day; weekdays are less busy than weekends.

## Zion–Mount Carmel Highway and the tunnel — MAJOR 2026 CHANGE
Sources: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/the-zion-mount-carmel-tunnel.htm (now titled "Large Vehicles")
and https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/2026-01-05-large-vehicles.htm (both fetched 2026-09-17)

- **Effective June 7, 2026**, vehicles exceeding any of these limits are **no longer permitted** on the highway
  between **Canyon Junction and the park's East Entrance** (a 10.7-mile route):
  - Length **35 ft 9 in**
  - Width **7 ft 10 in**
  - Height **11 ft 4 in**
  - Weight **50,000 lbs**
  - Combined vehicles: trailer no more than 26 ft, total no more than 50 ft.
- "If your vehicle exceeds the limits and you arrive at the park's South or East Entrances, you will be given
  instructions for turning around safely."
- Large vehicles may still enter via the South Entrance to use the large-vehicle lot near the Zion Canyon Visitor
  Center, visit Zion Lodge, drive the Scenic Drive in the shuttle off-season, or camp at Watchman/South.
- Alternate routes around the park to Bryce Canyon, the North Rim and elsewhere add **10 to 45 minutes**.
- **IMPORTANT CONFLICT / CHANGE:** the old **$15 tunnel escort permit** (8 a.m.–6 p.m. escorts, good for two trips
  in seven days, in place since October 4, 2010 per
  https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/zion-national-park-to-reinstate-tunnel-fees.htm) is **no longer described
  anywhere on the current Large Vehicles page** — the page has no mention of permits, fees or escorts, only the
  size ban. Reading the two together, the escort program appears to have been replaced by the outright restriction
  on June 7, 2026. The guide states the June 7, 2026 restriction as fact and does NOT quote a $15 escort fee.
  FLAGGED as a change readers may still see old advice about.
- Also prohibited in the tunnel historically: bicycles and pedestrians — NOT restated on the current page. FLAGGED.

## Services
- Park open "24 hours a day, 7 days a week, year-round."
- Zion Canyon Visitor Center: 8 a.m. – 5 p.m. Zion Human History Museum: 10 a.m. – 5 p.m.
  Kolob Canyons Visitor Center: 8 a.m. – 12 p.m. and 1 p.m. – 5 p.m.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/hours.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Rangers answer the phone (435-772-3256) 10 a.m. – 5 p.m. Mountain Time.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- **Zion Lodge "is the only place inside the park to purchase food."** Restrooms and water at The Grotto,
  Scout Lookout, the Visitor Center and the Riverside Walk trailhead.
  — lodging.htm; hike-angels-landing.htm; hike-riverside-walk.htm; hike-watchman-trail.htm
- Springdale has the groceries, gas, restaurants and outfitters (Narrows gear rental).
- UNVERIFIED: NPS's basicinfo.htm has an "Internet & Cellular Access" heading but the content did not render on
  fetch. Cell coverage described qualitatively in the guide (patchy in the canyon, better in Springdale) and
  flagged as not officially sourced. No gas station inside the park is confirmed by any page fetched.

- Park size **147,237 acres** and elevation range **3,700 ft (SW corner) to 8,726 ft (Horse Ranch Mountain)**
  — from NPS-sourced search results (Biophysical Description of Zion NP / Foundation Document), 2026-09-17.
  FLAGGED: not re-fetched from a single canonical page.

## Current conditions (as of 2026-09-17)
Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Park open year-round, 24 hours daily.
- **East Rim Trail partially closed due to rockfall near Weeping Rock.**
- **Hidden Canyon closed** (inaccessible because of the East Rim Trail closure).
- **Observation Point cannot be reached from Zion Canyon; only accessible from the east side.**
- **Toxic cyanobacteria advisory:** "Do not submerge your head in or drink instream water in Zion National Park."
- The Narrows closes when flow exceeds 150 cfs or during Flash Flood Warnings.
- Watchman open year-round; South and Lava Point close seasonally in winter; campgrounds "full almost every night
  mid-March through late November."
- Kolob Terrace Road "usually closed fall through spring due to snow"; Kolob Canyons Road "frequently closes in
  winter"; Lava Point Road "typically closed fall into spring".
- **No 2026-specific construction closure was listed on the conditions page.**
- Fire restrictions prohibiting campfires were in effect as of the May 2026 South Campground reopening release —
  status today UNVERIFIED (not restated on the conditions page). Not asserted in the guide.
