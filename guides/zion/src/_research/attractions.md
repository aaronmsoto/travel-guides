# Zion — Attractions research notes

All facts fetched live on **2026-09-17** unless marked UNVERIFIED.

## Official trail numbers (NPS "Things to Do" pages — these carry the park's own measured stats)

### Angels Landing
- Permit required for everyone hiking Angels Landing (past Scout Lookout, the chain section).
  Start time for the hike is at The Grotto. Rangers may check permits anywhere on the trail.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/angels-landing-hiking-permits.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Angels Landing Trail segment itself: "0.5 mi (0.3 km)", duration "1-2 Hours". Trailhead: "The Grotto (shuttle stop #6)".
  Drop-offs "up to 1,000 feet on each side". Trail "unpaved, rocky, and less than 3 ft (1 m) wide in many sections".
  Restrooms at Scout Lookout and The Grotto; water at The Grotto. Pets not allowed.
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-angels-landing.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
  - NOTE: NPS prints "0.5 mi (0.3 km)" — the metric conversion on the NPS page is internally inconsistent. Using the mile figure.
- Approach — West Rim Trail to Scout Lookout: distance "1.9 mi (3 km)" ONE WAY, elevation gain "1187 ft (361.8 m)",
  duration "3-4 Hours", difficulty "strenuous", typical grade 12.9% / max 43%. Walter's Wiggles = 21 switchbacks.
  Trailhead: "The Grotto (shuttle stop #6). Cross the road and over the bridge to begin the West Rim Trail."
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-to-scout-lookout.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Derived total used in guide: ~4.8 mi round trip (1.9 mi x 2 + 0.5 mi x 2). Only doubling of official one-way numbers; no invented figures.
- UNVERIFIED/CONFLICT: an NPS search summary also reported "Scout Lookout via West Rim Trail from The Grotto takes 3 hours,
  covers 4.2 mi / 6.2 km, with 1000 ft / 305 m elevation gain". This conflicts with the 1.9 mi one-way / 1187 ft page.
  Guide uses the Things-to-Do page numbers (1.9 mi one way, 1,187 ft) as the more specific/current source.

### The Narrows (bottom-up, from Temple of Sinawava)
- "You do not need a permit to hike the Narrows from the Temple of Sinawava going upstream in the Virgin River."
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/index.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Riverside Walk is "1 mile from the Temple of Sinawava" on paved trail; permit-free upstream travel as far as Big Spring;
  Big Spring is a "ten-mile round trip, all-day adventure".
  Gear: "Closed-toed shoes and a sturdy hiking stick", quick-drying layers.
  Top-down is a "16-mile through-hike" from Chamberlain's Ranch, permit required, own transportation.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/thenarrows.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Closure rule: park closes The Narrows when Virgin River flow exceeds **150 cubic feet per second (cfs)**, and during Flash Flood Warnings.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Water temps: summer 56–68°F; spring/fall 41–53°F; winter 38–45°F. Hypothermia is "the number one killer of
  outdoor recreationists, even in summer" and "a greater hazard in narrow canyons".
  Gear by season: neoprene socks/pants/bibs or wetsuit (spring/fall); wetsuit or drysuit (winter).
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/narrowssafety.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- UNVERIFIED: Temple of Sinawava is shuttle stop #9 per the shuttle-system stop list (confirmed there), but thenarrows.htm
  itself does not print the stop number. Wall Street mileage is NOT stated on any NPS page fetched — omitted from the guide.

### Riverside Walk
- Duration "1-2 Hours"; distance "1 mi (1.6 km)" one way; elevation gain "154.2 ft (47.0 m)"; paved, relatively flat.
  Trailhead at "Temple of Sinawava (shuttle stop #9)". Concrete surface, typical width 77 in, 4.6% grade; some irregular
  paving and minor drop-offs. Pets: "No". Restrooms and water at the trailhead. Winter closures possible due to ice.
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-riverside-walk.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)

### Emerald Pools
- Middle Emerald Pools Trail: duration "1-2 Hours", "2.2 miles (3.5 km) roundtrip (out-and-back)",
  elevation gain "332.8 ft (101.4 m)", moderate. "Emerald Pools Trailhead is across from the Zion Lodge (shuttle stop #5)".
  "The pools are fragile and an essential water source for wildlife. Do not go into the water." Open year-round.
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-middle-emerald-pools-trail.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Upper Emerald Pools Trail: duration "1-2 Hours", "0.3 mi (0.4 km)" one way, elevation gain "75.8 ft (23.11 m)", moderate.
  Reachable from "Zion Lodge (shuttle stop #5)" or "The Grotto (shuttle stop #6)". Swimming not allowed.
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-upper-emerald-pools.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Lower Emerald Pool Trail: UNVERIFIED — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-lower-emerald-pool-trail.htm returned only
  page chrome on fetch (2026-09-17); an NPS-derived search summary gave "1 hour, 1.2 mi / 1.9 km, 69 ft / 21 m elevation gain".
  Guide quotes the Middle/Upper numbers (verified) and describes Lower qualitatively only.

### Canyon Overlook Trail  (the "Overlook Trail" the owner asked for)
- Duration "1-2 Hours"; "1 mi (1.6 km)" total (0.5 mi one way); elevation gain "154.2 ft (47.0 m)", loss "48.5 ft (14.78 m)";
  moderate with steep sections, max grade 50.4%. East Side, via SR-9 through the Zion–Mount Carmel Tunnel.
  "Parking is extremely limited, be prepared to try mulitple times." [sic] No reservations required. Pets not allowed.
  Metal handrails; sandstone steps up to 18 in tall; overhanging rock with min 4 ft clearance; tread 12–48 in.
  Views of the Towers of the Virgin; fenced cliff edge at trail's end. "Keep an eye out for bighorn sheep along the trail."
  Pit toilets at the parking area.
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-canyon-overlook.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)

### Watchman Trail
- Duration "1-2 Hours"; "3.3 miles (5.3 km) roundtrip"; elevation gain "479.4 ft (146.1 m)"; moderate.
  Trailhead from the Zion Canyon Visitor Center plaza, past the shuttle stop, along the river.
  Views of the Watchman, lower Zion Canyon, and Springdale. Year-round; muddy/icy in winter and monsoon; little summer shade.
  Bicycles and pets not permitted.
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-watchman-trail.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)

### Pa'rus Trail
- Duration "30-120 Minutes"; "3.5 mile (5.6 km) from one end to the other"; elevation gain "151.7 ft (46.3 m)";
  typical grade 2.6%, max 11.4%. Trailheads: Zion Canyon Visitor Center (stop #1) or Canyon Junction (stop #3).
  "93.6% of the trail is paved and 6.4% is hard wood decking"; wheelchair accessible; min tread width 96 in.
  Bicycles allowed. Pets allowed on a leash "no longer than 6 feet" — the ONLY pet-friendly trail in Zion.
  "Pa'rus" means "bubbling water" in Paiute. Good sunset/stargazing.
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-pa-rus-trail.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)

### Observation Point via East Mesa Trail
- "6 miles (9.6 km) to the East Rim Trail and back. 6.8 miles (11 km) to Observation Point and back."
  Elevation change "300 ft / 90 m". "3 hours to Observation Point and back."
  Trailhead "only accessible by high-clearance / 4-wheel-drive dirt roads off the North Fork Road."
  "Roads can be extremely muddy and inaccessible in the winter, spring, and after rainstorms."
  "Only park in designated spaces. Do not park on private property. Illegally parked vehicles may be cited or towed."
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/east-rim-wilderness-trail-descriptions.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- NPS does not print a difficulty rating for East Mesa. Guide labels it "moderate" and says so is an editorial call — flagged.
- Observation Point CANNOT be reached from Zion Canyon; East Rim Trail is partially closed by rockfall near Weeping Rock.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)

### Zion Canyon Scenic Drive / Court of the Patriarchs
- Court of the Patriarchs is "shuttle stop #4". "A short, but steep, walk to the Court of the Patriarchs overlook."
  Three peaks: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob — "named in 1916 by a Methodist Minister, Dr. Frederick Vining Fischer,
  and two local boys from Southern Utah".
  — https://www.nps.gov/places/court-of-the-patriarchs.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Shuttle stops in order: 1 Zion Canyon Visitor Center, 2 Zion Human History Museum, 3 Canyon Junction,
  4 Court of the Patriarchs, 5 Zion Lodge, 6 The Grotto, 7 Weeping Rock, 8 Big Bend, 9 Temple of Sinawava.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/zion-canyon-shuttle-system.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- "Inside the park, shuttles take about 45 minutes to drive between the Zion Canyon Visitor Center and the
  Temple of Sinawava, and a round trip through Zion Canyon takes about an hour and a half."
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/2026-02-06-shuttle-schedule.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- UNVERIFIED: the commonly cited "6.6 miles" length of the Zion Canyon Scenic Drive was NOT found on any NPS page
  fetched today — omitted from the guide.

### Zion–Mount Carmel Highway / Checkerboard Mesa
- The restricted section is "between Canyon Junction and the park's East Entrance" on a "10.7-mile route".
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/2026-01-05-large-vehicles.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- "The Zion-Mt. Carmel Highway was officially dedicated and opened to the public on July 3, 1930"; its completion made
  the "Grand Circle Tour" of Zion, Bryce Canyon and Grand Canyon possible.
  — https://www.nps.gov/history/hdp/exhibits/parkitect/tran/zion03.htm (via NPS search result, 2026-09-17)
- "East of Zion Canyon, along the Zion-Mt. Carmel Highway, unique sandstone formations can be viewed from scenic
  overlooks and trails." Checkerboard Mesa has a wayside/overlook along the highway.
  — nps.gov search results (2026-09-17)
- UNVERIFIED: tunnel length (commonly given as 1.1 mi) was not on any page fetched — omitted.

### Kolob Canyons (alsoConsider)
- "Located at Exit 40 on Interstate 15, 40 miles north of Zion Canyon and 17 miles south of Cedar City."
  "A five-mile scenic drive along the Kolob Canyons Road". "over 20 miles of hiking trails".
  "2,000 foot cliff walls". "The Kolob Canyons Road may be closed during winter months due to snow or ice."
  Kolob Canyons Visitor Center: open 8 am–12 pm and 1 pm–5 pm.
  — https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/kolob-canyons.htm and .../hours.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)
- Timber Creek Overlook Trail: duration "30-60 Minutes", "0.6 mi (1 km)" one way, elevation gain "117.3 ft (35.8 m)",
  "short, relatively easy" with "minimal elevation gain". "From the Kolob Canyons Visitor Center, follow the
  Kolob Canyons Road to the very end." Views of Shuntavi Butte, Timber Creek, Kolob Terrace, Pine Valley Mountains.
  — https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/timber-creek-overlook.htm (fetched 2026-09-17)

## Ranking rationale (top 10, most popular first)
1 Angels Landing — the park's signature hike and the only one with a lottery.
2 The Narrows (bottom-up) — the other bucket-list hike; no permit, so volume is enormous.
3 Riverside Walk — paved, at the end of the shuttle line, and the gateway to the Narrows; near-universal.
4 Emerald Pools — the classic family hike from Zion Lodge, shuttle stop #5.
5 Canyon Overlook Trail — the best short big-view hike and the main draw on the east side.
6 Zion Canyon Scenic Drive + Court of the Patriarchs — nearly every visitor rides it.
7 Zion–Mount Carmel Highway / Checkerboard Mesa — the scenic drive everyone takes toward Bryce.
8 Pa'rus Trail — paved, pet- and bike-friendly, right at the visitor center.
9 Watchman Trail — the best short canyon hike you can start without a shuttle.
10 Observation Point via East Mesa — the highest-value big view still open after the Weeping Rock rockfall.

Closed / deliberately excluded: Weeping Rock area trails, Hidden Canyon, Observation Point from Zion Canyon
(East Rim Trail partially closed by rockfall). Kolob Canyons and The Subway moved to alsoConsider.
